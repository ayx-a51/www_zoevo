Privacy policy

Last updated 21 November 2026

zoevo is a habit, focus and routine tracker. It works without an account, carries no ads, doesn’t measure how you use it, and keeps what you track to itself. This page sets out exactly what is stored, where, and how to delete it.

  • Works without an accountAs a guest, nothing you track is sent to us.
  • Optional sign-inBacks up and syncs your data through Google Firebase.
  • No ads, no trackingNo crash reporting, and how you use zoevo isn’t measured.
  • Delete it yourselfRight in the app, whenever you like.

Who we are

zoevo is an Android, iPhone and iPad app for daily and weekly goals, focus sessions, counters, stopwatches and routines. It is published by A51. This policy covers the zoevo app and this website, zoevo.io; “we” means A51. For any question about it, write to hi@zoevo.io.

Using zoevo without an account

zoevo needs no account. Until you sign in (the app calls this guest mode), everything you create is kept in the app’s private storage on your device:

  • your sources (focus sessions, counters and stopwatches), your daily and weekly goals and your routines, with the names, icons, colours, targets, planned weekdays and reminder times you give them;
  • your history: what you log each day, which days were planned, skipped or missed, and the routines you completed;
  • your settings, and, if you start the free trial, an encoded record of its start and end dates so it can’t be started again on that device.

None of this is sent to us. Reminders, timer notifications, alarms and widgets all work on the device itself. Uninstalling zoevo removes its data from the device, though a copy may remain in your device backup (see below) and in any backup file you saved (see Backup files).

Android device backup

zoevo doesn’t switch off Android’s own app backup. If backup is turned on for your phone, Android may include zoevo’s on-device data (your guest data, the trial record and the offline copy of synced data) in the backup it keeps in your Google account, and restore it when you install zoevo again. That backup is run by Android and Google, not by us, and we can’t see it. You can turn it off or delete it in your phone’s Google backup settings.

iPhone and iPad backup

zoevo doesn’t leave its data out of Apple’s device backup. If you back up your iPhone or iPad to iCloud or to a computer, the backup may hold zoevo’s on-device data, such as your guest data, the trial record and your settings, and bring it back when you restore your device from it. Apple runs iCloud Backup, and a computer backup stays on your computer; we can’t see either. You can leave zoevo out of iCloud Backup, or delete an iCloud backup, in your device’s iCloud settings.

Signing in with Apple or Google

Signing in is optional. It backs up your data and keeps it in sync across your devices. On iPhone and iPad you can sign in with Apple or Google. On Android you sign in with Google and, from version 1.1.0, with Apple too.

What your Google account shares

zoevo uses Google Sign-In with Firebase Authentication. Google passes on your email address, your name, a link to your profile picture and your Google account ID. Firebase keeps them with a random zoevo user ID and the dates the account was created and last signed in. The app shows your name and email in its settings and keeps a small copy of your profile picture on the device, so it still appears offline; that copy is removed when you sign out. We never see your Google password.

What your Apple Account shares

zoevo uses Sign in with Apple through Firebase Authentication. The first time you sign in, Apple asks what to share: your name, and your email address or, if you choose Hide My Email, a private relay address that Apple forwards to your own. Apple passes on what you choose, together with the account ID it uses for you in zoevo. Firebase keeps them with a random zoevo user ID and the dates the account was created and last signed in. Apple shares no profile picture, and we never see your Apple Account password.

If you sign in with Apple and with Google under the same email address, zoevo can link both to one account, so either one opens it.

What your account stores

While you are signed in, your data is saved in Google Cloud Firestore under your user ID instead of only on the device:

  • Sources: focus sessions, counters and stopwatches with their name, icon, colour, settings such as session length, archive history, and the live state of a running stopwatch so your other devices can show it.
  • Goals: daily and weekly goals with the source they count, the Aim (minimum) or Cap (maximum) target, any stretch goal, planned weekdays, reminder time and whether the reminder is on.
  • Routines: their activities, order and durations, planned weekdays and reminder time.
  • History: the values logged for each source per day (focus sessions, counts, stopwatch time), each day’s plan with skipped and missed marks, and every completed routine run with its time and duration.
  • Settings: the day your planning week starts, the plan-the-week reminder, and internal markers such as when your weekly plans were last rolled forward.
  • zoevo+ status: see zoevo+, the trial and promo codes.

If your account is still empty when you first sign in, the data you created as a guest is moved into it. Firestore also keeps a copy on the device, so zoevo works offline and syncs when you are back online.

What we use it for

Only to run zoevo: to show your goals, streaks and statistics, to sync and restore them, and to know whether zoevo+ is active. We don’t use your data for advertising or profiling, and we don’t sell it.

What we don’t collect

No precise location, contacts, photos or files, no camera or microphone, and no advertising ID. zoevo doesn’t look at other apps on your device. When you sign in with Google, Google may use your IP address to estimate roughly where you are, to prevent fraud; the App Store lists this as coarse location.

zoevo+, the trial and promo codes

  • Subscriptions on Android are sold and billed by Google Play, and we never see your payment details. Buying there needs you to be signed in. The app hands your zoevo user ID to Google Play with the purchase so it can be matched to your account. Our server then asks Google Play to confirm the purchase and saves in your account the plan (monthly or yearly), its status, the expiry date Google Play reports, whether it renews, whether it was a test purchase, and the purchase token Google Play issues. So that one purchase unlocks one account, the server also keeps a record of which user ID a purchase token belongs to. It checks the purchase with Google Play again from time to time, so a cancelled or refunded subscription ends in the app too. The server also counts how many purchase checks each account asked for in the last hour.
  • Subscriptions on iPhone and iPad are sold and billed by Apple through the App Store, and we never see your payment details. You can buy without an account: zoevo+ then works on the iPhones and iPads that use the Apple Account that paid, and is added to your account the first time you sign in. If you buy while signed in, the app adds a code worked out from your zoevo user ID (Apple calls it an app account token), so the purchase can be matched to your account. Our server checks each purchase with Apple and saves in your account the plan (monthly or yearly), its status, the expiry date, whether it renews, whether it was a test purchase, and the transaction IDs Apple issues. So that one purchase unlocks one account, the server also keeps a record of each subscription: the user ID it belongs to, that code, the plan, its status and dates. Apple’s server tells ours when a subscription renews, runs into a billing problem, expires or is refunded, so a cancelled or refunded subscription ends in the app too. A subscription bought without an account gets the same record, naming no account until you sign in. As on Android, the server counts how many purchase checks each account asked for in the last hour.
  • Across platforms: once you’re signed in, zoevo+ follows your account. A Google Play subscription also works on iPhone and iPad, and, from version 1.1.0, an App Store subscription also works on Android.
  • The 14-day free trial starts in the app and needs no account. Your device keeps an encoded record of its start and end so it runs once per device. If you are signed in, your account also notes when the trial started, so it runs once per account.
  • Promo codes are entered on Android. They need you to be signed in and are checked on our server. Redeeming one saves the code and when you redeemed it under your user ID, together with the zoevo+ status it gives you. To stop codes from being guessed, the server counts how many codes each account tried in the last hour. On iPhone and iPad, Redeem code takes Apple’s offer codes instead, which count as an App Store purchase.

Backup files

Under Settings › Offline backup you can save all your zoevo data to a single file (with zoevo+) and import such a file again later, on the same device or another one, Android, iPhone or iPad (free for everyone).

  • What’s in it: your sources, goals, routines, their history and your week-planning settings, as described in Using zoevo without an account and Signing in with Apple or Google. It doesn’t contain your email, name or profile picture, your zoevo+ status, the trial record, promo codes or the device’s display settings.
  • Where it goes: wherever you choose: in Android’s own save dialog, for example your phone’s storage or a cloud drive, or on iPhone and iPad in the Files dialog, for example On My iPhone or iCloud Drive. zoevo writes the file only there and never sends it to us. The file isn’t encrypted, so anyone who can open it can read your data: keep it somewhere private.
  • Importing replaces all the data currently in zoevo, on the device or, if you’re signed in, in your account, with the contents of the file you pick in Android’s file dialog or the Files dialog. zoevo reads only that one file.

Services we rely on

  • Google Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Firestore and Cloud Functions): sign-in and your synced data once you sign in, and the server that checks zoevo+ purchases and promo codes and receives Apple’s notices about App Store subscriptions. The server runs in Google’s Zurich region (europe-west6).
  • Firebase App Check, with Google Play Integrity on Android (from version 1.1.0) and Apple’s App Attest on iPhone and iPad: Google Play or Apple vouches that the app is a genuine copy of zoevo, so our server and database can check where requests come from. It runs when zoevo starts, also without an account, and sends nothing you track.
  • Google Sign-In: signing in with your Google account.
  • Sign in with Apple (Apple): signing in with your Apple Account, on iPhone and iPad and, from version 1.1.0, on Android.
  • Google Play: installing zoevo on Android, billing zoevo+ there, and confirming those purchases to our server.
  • The App Store (Apple): installing zoevo on iPhone and iPad, billing zoevo+ there, and confirming those purchases to our server (see zoevo+, the trial and promo codes).
  • Android backup (Google): if you have device backup turned on, Android may back up zoevo’s on-device data to your Google account (see Android device backup).
  • iCloud Backup (Apple): if you back up your iPhone or iPad to iCloud, iOS may include zoevo’s on-device data (see iPhone and iPad backup).
  • Google Fonts (Android, up to version 1.0.0): zoevo downloads its typefaces (Roboto and Azeret Mono) from Google’s font servers the first time it needs them and keeps them on the phone. Like any download, this shows Google your IP address; none of your data goes with it. From version 1.1.0, on Android, iPhone and iPad alike, the typefaces are part of the app and nothing is downloaded.
  • GitHub Pages: hosts this website.

When the app connects to these services, their servers see your device’s IP address, as with any internet connection. Google handles data under the Google Privacy Policy, Apple under the Apple Privacy Policy. We share your data with no one else, and we don’t sell it.

No ads, no tracking

zoevo contains no advertising, no analytics library (neither Firebase Analytics nor Google Analytics), no crash reporting and no third-party tracking, and it doesn’t read your advertising ID. How you use the app is not measured or reported. The Firebase libraries that sign you in and sync your data do send Google basic technical details with their requests (the device model, the operating-system and app versions and the library’s own version), which Google uses, without linking them to you, to see which versions are in use. The App Store lists this as diagnostic data used for analytics.

This website sets no cookies and loads nothing from other servers. GitHub, which hosts it, logs visitors’ IP addresses for security, as web hosts do.

Permissions

zoevo asks for no location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or storage access. Saving or importing a backup goes through Android’s own file dialog or the Files dialog on iPhone and iPad, which lets zoevo open only the one file you pick. zoevo uses the internet for sign-in, sync, purchases and App Check (and, on Android up to version 1.0.0, fonts), and checks whether you are online before backing up, deleting or replacing synced data.

On Android

  • Notifications: reminders for goals, routines and planning your week, and the pause and continue controls for running focus sessions and stopwatches.
  • Alarms and reminders (exact alarms): so a focus session or timer ends right on time.
  • Run at startup: to restore scheduled reminders after your phone restarts.

On iPhone and iPad

  • Notifications: reminders for goals, routines and planning your week, and the alerts when a focus session ends or a stopwatch reaches its goal. Those alerts are marked Time Sensitive, so they can reach you during a Focus such as Do Not Disturb.
  • Alarms (iOS 26 and later): so a focus session ends with an alarm, also in Silent mode, and shows its countdown on the Lock Screen.
  • Live Activities: running stopwatches show on the Lock Screen. You can turn them off for zoevo in the Settings app.

Security

Synced data is encrypted on its way to Google (HTTPS), and Google stores it encrypted. The database rules let a signed-in user read and write only their own records. Guest data lives in the app’s private storage, which other apps can’t read.

How long we keep it, and deleting it

Your data is kept for as long as you use zoevo: on the device until you delete it or uninstall the app, and in your account until you delete it or the account. On iPhone and iPad, iOS keeps zoevo’s sign-in in the keychain when you uninstall zoevo, so installing it again signs you straight back in; sign out first if you don’t want that. A copy in your Android device backup, if you use one, is kept and deleted by Google according to your backup settings; a copy in an iPhone or iPad backup stays until that backup is replaced or deleted. A backup file you saved stays wherever you put it until you delete it; deleting data in the app doesn’t touch it.

In the app, Settings › Data deletion offers Delete timeline, Delete all data and Delete account, on Android, iPhone and iPad alike. The Data deletion page explains each one, and how to ask us by email instead.

Delete account removes your synced data and your sign-in. It doesn’t cancel zoevo+: cancel a Google Play subscription in the Play Store under Payments & subscriptions, and an App Store subscription in your Apple Account settings (in the Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions).

If you signed in with Apple, Apple asks you to confirm the deletion, and once you have, zoevo revokes the tokens Apple issued to it for your account.

A few zoevo+ records stay on our server after Delete account: which Google Play purchases and App Store subscriptions belong to the account, its zoevo+ status and trial start, the promo codes it redeemed, and the counts of its recent purchase and code checks. The app can’t delete them and our server doesn’t delete them with the account. The purchase records stay so that one purchase can’t unlock a second account. They are filed under the deleted account’s random user ID and hold no name, no email address and nothing you tracked.

Requests by email are completed within 5 business days. Our own server backups may be kept securely for up to 365 days for operational reasons.

User groups

Everyone is treated the same. We don’t ask for your age, and we don’t collect different data or apply different rules for different groups of users.

Your rights

With zoevo+ you can save a copy of your data yourself as a backup file (see Backup files). You can also ask us for a copy of the data held in your account, ask us to correct it, or have it deleted, either yourself in the app or by writing to hi@zoevo.io. Depending on where you live, data-protection law may give you further rights, and we will honour them.

Changes and contact

If what zoevo collects changes, we will update this page and the date at the top of it.
